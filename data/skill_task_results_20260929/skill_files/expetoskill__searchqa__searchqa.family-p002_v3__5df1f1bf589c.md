1. **Interpret Descriptive Clues**: When a question provides attributes, titles, or functions instead of a direct name (e.g., 'King of...', 'sound made by...'), extract key semantic phrases to form the initial search query. Do not assume the answer is a proper noun; it may be a common noun, number, or term.
2. **Use Exact Phrases for Titles/Epithets**: For questions involving specific nicknames or titles (e.g., 'King of the Techno-Thriller', 'Gibraltar in Cleats'), include the phrase in quotes in the search query to target biographical summaries, obituaries, or authoritative sources that explicitly attribute the nickname.
3. **Cross-Reference Attribution**: When a title search yields multiple sources, verify the subject's identity by cross-referencing general news snippets with biographical summaries (e.g., Wikipedia, ANB) to ensure the attribution is consistent and accurate before finalizing the answer.
4. **Search for Functional Definitions**: For questions asking for a term defined by a function or analogy (e.g., 'canvas-covered blades of a windmill'), include domain qualifiers (e.g., 'nautical') in the query to filter out irrelevant mechanical or engineering definitions.
5. **Verify Specific Associations**: When verifying that an ingredient or property belongs to a specific item (e.g., 'rubber in chewing gum'), search for the specific item combined with the attribute (e.g., 'bubble gum rubber ingredient') to find direct confirmation rather than general statements.
6. **Handle Multi-Medium Roles**: For questions about actors playing specific roles across different media (film vs. TV), include both mediums in the search query (e.g., 'actress played Tina Turner film Rosa Parks TV') to capture the full scope of the role history.
7. **Account for Total Counts in Biology**: When searching for biological counts (e.g., 'number of eyes on a bee'), be aware that results focusing only on major features (e.g., 'compound eyes') may undercount. Look for explicit total counts that break down types (e.g., '5 eyes' including ocelli) to provide the complete answer.
8. **Prioritize Origin Stories for Cultural Facts**: For questions about cultural or literary associations (e.g., 'birds choose mates on this holiday'), prioritize sources that mention origin stories or specific literary references (e.g., Chaucer) over general historical overviews to confirm the specific tradition.
9. **Verify Geographic/Product Links**: When identifying a country or region based on a product (e.g., 'red wines from Coonawarra'), search for the region name combined with the product type to confirm its reputation and location relative to known landmarks (e.g., 'southeast of Adelaide').
10. **Identify Primary Sources in Lists**: When source texts list multiple potential answers (e.g., animals for catgut) but highlight one as primary or typical (e.g., 'particularly sheep'), use that highlighted entity for the final answer rather than attempting to verify all listed options individually.
11. **Resolve Slogans to Full Names**: When a question involves a political slogan or campaign button (e.g., 'I Like Ike'), search using the slogan to identify the figure, but always submit the person's full formal name (e.g., 'Dwight D. Eisenhower') as the final answer, avoiding nicknames or partial names which are often rejected.
12. **Account for Nicknames in Queries**: When searching for historical figures via slogans or informal references, account for nicknames (e.g., 'Ike') in the query rather than relying solely on full legal names to ensure relevant results are retrieved.
13. **Avoid Pattern Matching for City Lists**: When the input consists of city names (e.g., Minsk, Monrovia, Manama), avoid treating them as a pattern to match against other lists. Instead, look for passages that explicitly link each city to its country (e.g., 'Minsk Belarus') to identify the correct entity type or relationship.
14. **Leverage Etymology for Word Origins**: For questions asking for a term derived from a specific language or literal meaning (e.g., 'Latin for before behind'), search for the etymological breakdown or dictionary definition of the root components to identify the target adjective or noun.
15. **Confirm Musical/Artistic Terms via Definition**: When a question describes a free-form, emotional, or improvisational artistic composition, search for the specific genre or term (e.g., 'Rhapsody', 'Improvisation') using the descriptive keywords provided in the clue.
16. **Identify Historical Currency by Value**: When identifying a historical currency unit defined by its fraction of a pound or pre-decimal status, search for the specific coin name associated with that value (e.g., 'one-twentieth of a pound') rather than just the date or country.
17. **Locate Literary Works via Critical Reception**: When searching for a specific encyclopedia's description of a literary work, include the name of the encyclopedia and key descriptive phrases (e.g., 'greatest poem') in the search query to locate the exact source text or critical consensus.
18. **Solve Wordplay/Riddles via Role-Attribute Combination**: When solving wordplay or riddle-based questions, prioritize search queries that combine the specific role or noun with the key action or attribute mentioned in the prompt (e.g., 'bouncer bad check') to find direct definitions or puns.
19. **Distinguish Character from Actor in Film Trivia**: When a question implies identifying the character or entity defined by the roles (e.g., 'destroyer' vs 'barbarian'), search for the character name or franchise title first. Avoid answering with the actor's name unless the question explicitly asks for the performer.
20. **Refine Multi-Word Answers on Rejection**: If a multi-word answer (e.g., 'bear market') is rejected, attempt submitting the core single-word term (e.g., 'bear') or a shortened variant, as some tasks require concise terms rather than full phrases.
